## Result first

`v4-cellattn-labels` is below the best booster on every one of the 10 datasets (average precision, AP) and ranks 4th
or 5th of 6 arms on every dataset. It is above its untrained control on all 10, though by only 0.0206 and 0.0211 AP
on Higgs and Diabetes130US. Mean AP over the 10 datasets: 0.7478 for the checkpoint, 0.7629 logistic regression,
0.8496 CatBoost, 0.8441 LightGBM, 0.8377 XGBoost, 0.5784 untrained control.

**The spread did not come out the way §44 predicted.** Standard deviation of per-dataset AP over the 10 datasets is
0.1034 for the checkpoint against 0.0989 (LightGBM), 0.1005 (CatBoost) and 0.1071 (XGBoost): the checkpoint's spread
is inside the boosters' range. The n = 4 reading in the earlier partial draft (0.1510 against about 0.09) did not
survive the other six datasets. What differs is the level, not the spread: the checkpoint trails CatBoost by 0.1018
AP on average (std of the gap 0.0767, range 0.0085 to 0.2244), and the gap varies a lot by dataset.

All ten datasets have base rate 0.500 (the benchmark balances classes), so AP and ROC-AUC give the same ordering;
AP is first per `CLAUDE.md`. No claim about imbalanced data follows from this suite.

## Caveats that apply to every number below

- **Context versus training fold.** The in-context arms see at most `max_context` = 1,000 labelled rows; logistic
  regression and the boosters fit the whole training fold (about two thirds of the rows used, so about 7,000 to
  20,000 rows). The comparison is therefore not at equal training rows. The gaps measure this checkpoint with this
  context size, not a ceiling on in-context learning.
- **Row caps.** 6 of 10 datasets are subsampled to 30,000 rows (stratified, fixed cap seed): MiniBooNE, road-safety,
  Higgs, Diabetes130US and both covertype files (see the `rows used (of)` column). The other four use all rows.
- **Categoricals as integer codes.** `to_float_matrix` maps categorical columns to codes for every arm, so the
  `clf_cat/*` files are scored on codes, not one-hot or native categoricals; the boosters' native categorical
  handling is not used.
- **One seed, 3 folds.** No seed-to-seed variance is measured. Fold ranges of the checkpoint (max minus min fold AP)
  are 0.0044 to 0.0173 per dataset.
- **Binary versions of the benchmark's files.** Base rate 0.500 on all ten (including the two covertype files and
  the credit-card pair) is as the loader returns them.

Caution on what this standard deviation measures: it is dominated by how hard each dataset is (MiniBooNE and
Diabetes130US sit at the extremes for every arm), so two arms can share a spread while differing in level. A
dataset-difficulty-free spread is the std of the gap to the best booster: 0.0767 AP (n = 10, range 0.0085 to
0.2244, CatBoost reference). The suite has no boosters-only counterpart of that statistic (a booster's gap to the
best booster is 0 by construction for the best one), so no like-for-like spread comparison is possible beyond the
table above.

## Oddities and checks

- **No failures, skips or NaN.** All 10 exit 0; every log ends `coverage: 18/18 (dataset, fold, arm) units scored,
  0 skipped`; merged coverage 180/180. No single-class split. No dataset skipped for width (widest: 54 features,
  `clf_cat/covertype`). No time limit hit (largest 4,553 s against a 12 h limit).
- **Untrained control is not at chance everywhere.** AP 0.7221 (MagicTelescope), 0.7006 (bank-marketing), 0.6129
  (Diabetes130US) against 0.4957 to 0.5775 on the rest. A random network ranks above chance on datasets with a
  simple signal, so "above control" is a per-dataset statement and not proof of what pretraining contributes.
- **Warnings only.** sklearn `OptimizeWarning: Unknown solver options: iprint` in the logistic-regression arm, 18
  three per dataset (one per fold) in every log; no effect on results.
- **Commit `e475151`**: its subject concerns other work (multiclass floor, regression arms); the kernels pin it for
  the `suite_scoring` module. Confirm before quoting the command as reproduced.
- **Machine-time estimate of the task note.** The note's ESTIMATE (7 to 10 hours for all ten at 2 threads under
  load) is not comparable with the Kaggle numbers: the 10 datasets took 30,614 s of kernel time summed (about 8.5 h)
  on 4 threads over 4 kernels; the two fintfm arms 14,975 s and 15,095 s summed over datasets and folds, the three
  boosters and logreg 465 s together.

## 37.5 (the suite as the standing gate): what the evidence supports, decision open

Supports a gate: (1) the suite separates the checkpoint from the control and from the boosters on most datasets, with
the gap to CatBoost larger than the checkpoint's own fold range on 9 of 10 datasets (the exception is Diabetes130US,
0.0085 against 0.0158), and the margin over the control larger than the control's fold range on 9 of 10 (the exception is Higgs, 0.0206
against 0.0207); (2) it is cheap to run (about 8.5 kernel hours summed,
4 kernels, CPU only) and resumable per dataset; (3) a benchmark one column solves cannot diagnose anything (§42) and
this suite has per-dataset structure (the encoding sensitivity on covertype, the near-flat Diabetes130US).

Counts against or leaves open: (1) one seed and 3 folds, so a before-and-after difference smaller than about 0.02 AP
per dataset cannot be resolved from fold ranges; (2) the headline "spread" statistic did not discriminate the
checkpoint from the boosters, so a gate should use per-dataset AP and the mean gap to a booster, not spread alone;
(3) the in-context arms are capped at 1,000 context rows, which confounds any prior change with context use;
(4) all base rates are 0.500, so the suite says nothing about imbalanced data, the project's low-base-rate use case;
(5) the untrained control is far from chance on three datasets. No recommendation is made here.
