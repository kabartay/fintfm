## Result 1: the "same positives recur" finding of section 194 is a diagnostic artefact

`scripts/retrieval_plan_diag.py` built the retrieval plan from the raw (standardised) test
rows. `FinancialTFMClassifier` stores its pool **after** feature conditioning, and
`predict_proba` conditions the queries (`_select_query`, then `_condition`) before planning.
The diagnostic therefore placed unconditioned queries in the conditioned pool's distance
space. Same subsample, same fold:

| plan built from | pooled context rate (pool 0.3767%) | positives entering any context | median groups per entering positive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| raw queries (as section 194) | **4.0394%** | 116 of 120 | **52 of 64** |
| conditioned queries (as scoring does) | 0.6403% | 120 of 120 | 5 of 63 |

`predict_proba` itself reported `pooled_context_rate_` 0.640% on the scored arm, matching the
conditioned plan, not section 194's 9.74%. So there is **no hub effect** in the scoring path:
the 27-fold excess of context defaults and the 540-positives-in-136-groups recurrence do not
occur there. Section 194's cause paragraph is not supported; the AP collapse itself is real
(it reproduces below), only that explanation is withdrawn. `scripts/retrieval_plan_diag.py` is
fixed to condition the queries (its saved `diag_fold0.txt` is from the wrong version and has not
been re-run: a full-panel run was out of scope).

## Result 2: retrieval still hurts on the subsample, and the distance space is degenerate

AP first (fold 0, 44 test positives):

| arm | AP | ROC-AUC | mean predicted vs observed 0.4120% | context positive rate | wall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| default (uniform, `n_ensemble` 1, `max_context` 2000) | **0.1856** | 0.9868 | 0.7367% | n/a | 156-317 s |
| retrieval as shipped | 0.0943 | 0.9751 | 0.4844% | 0.640% | 652-762 s |
| retrieval, zero-IQR columns scaled by standard deviation | 0.0528 | 0.9586 | 0.8233% | 0.640% | 483 s |
| retrieval, `retrieval_per_class=8` (8 nearest positives) | 0.0127 | 0.8436 | 0.5171% | 0.400% | 659 s |
| 0.5 default + 0.5 retrieval (mean probability) | 0.1469 | 0.9853 | n/a | n/a | no extra pass |
| **`n_ensemble` 8 + `max_context` 4000, uniform, no retrieval** | **0.1989** | 0.9887 | 0.5784% | n/a | 2,731 s |

The shipped retrieval arm reproduced to four decimals across two runs (0.0943 twice); the
level is fine (mean predicted 0.48% against 0.41% observed), so this is a ranking loss, as
section 194 also found.

**The distance space is dominated by ten constant-ish columns.** 10 of 130 features have an
interquartile range of 0, so `distance_stats` hands them the 1e-6 scale floor and a deviation
of 1 becomes 1e6. Five of them hold 89.8% of the squared distance (`legal_form`,
`operational_status`, `incorporation_date_2`, `Insolvency_flag`, `Loss_flag`, ...). Retrieval
is therefore close to "match the categorical flags", and the 63 group targets (means of
conditioned queries) are fractional off-manifold points (median norm 5.0e6 against 1.9e6 for a
single query). With the standard-deviation fallback no column dominates (top five hold 18.3%).

**But fixing the scale does not fix retrieval**: AP falls from 0.0943 to 0.0528. The flag
match was carrying part of the signal (positives all carry `Insolvency_flag`, 100% beyond one
unit against 33% of negatives), and a metric without it loses that. Retrieval hurts at the
pool and fold measured for a reason that is not the scale floor, not a hub, and (section 194)
not `retrieval_min_positive`: with 120 positives, expected positives in a blind context are
7.5; the nearest-2000 draw gives about 4.8 (0.2403% on real-query targets), so the floor of
8 plausibly adds positives the local neighbourhood did not earn (context rate 0.640%); the
number of groups sitting at the floor was not counted.

## Result 3: the cheap fixes tested

- Scale fix (`std_fallback_stats`): AP 0.0528, worse than shipped retrieval (0.0943).
- Base-rate-matched positive count via `retrieval_per_class=8`: AP 0.0127, far worse. The 8
  nearest positives to a group target are chosen with no distance cut-off, so a distant
  positive enters a context of nearest negatives; the contrast the model reads is wrong.
  (Whether these 8 recur across groups was not measured here.)
- Ensembling retrieval with the uniform arm (mean probability): AP 0.1469, below the default
  0.1856. Blending with a worse ranker costs AP.
- Capping reuse of a positive: **not run**, because Result 1 removes the recurrence it was
  meant to cure (median 5 groups per positive, none above 63).

No tested fix makes retrieval beat the default, and the plain levers do:
`n_ensemble` 8 + `max_context` 4000 reaches AP 0.1989 against 0.1856 (+0.0133, one fold, 44
positives; inside the noise stated above).

## Against the Verify

Task 38.13 asks that, if a mechanism is found and fixable, retrieval (fixed) be re-measured
against `n_ensemble` + `max_context` alone, and otherwise the regime dependence be recorded.
Met in part: the re-measure of the unfixed and one fixed variant against `n_ensemble` 8 +
`max_context` 4000 is here (retrieval 0.0943 and 0.0528 against 0.1989), but on one fold of a
60,000-row subsample with 44 positives, not five folds. The mechanism is only half found: the
section 194 mechanism is withdrawn and a distance-space defect is found, but the reason
retrieval loses ranking even with a sound scale is not isolated. The task stays open.
