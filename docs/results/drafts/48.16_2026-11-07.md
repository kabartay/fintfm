# 48.16 surrogate method test on recorded runs (draft, 2026-11-07)

No GPU, no new run. Script `scripts/colid_surrogate.py`, tests `tests/test_colid_surrogate.py`
(5 passed). Not a FINDINGS entry; the coordinator numbers those.

## Data (MEASURED)

Twelve already-scored checkpoints, one score: V4FinBench five-fold mean AP, full 1,000,087-row
horizon-0 panel, `n_ensemble=8`, uniform context. Only this regime is used, because AP is not
comparable across row sets or inference settings. Excluded: the `n_ensemble=1` runs, the
prior-variant arms (priorctl, scm50, treeclean) and the fine-tuned arms. Each embedded AP is
re-checked against its run's `v4_protocol.json` (`--runs-dir`; all 12 match).

Inputs: `column_id_dim` (0 for the pre-cell-attention architecture), cell attention yes/no,
`max_context` (k rows), training tasks (k). One input (tasks_k = 24 for the §84 "A" arm) is
ESTIMATED: 6,000 steps at batch 4, the step count not being stated where I read it.

Only 12 rows exist, not about 15, and 5 of them are one axis (§104).

## Leave-one-out mean absolute error (AP, MEASURED inputs)

| set | n | mean predictor | ridge | GBM (50 trees, depth 2) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| all rows | 12 | 0.0196 | 0.0082 | 0.0041 |
| cell-attention rows only | 9 | 0.0069 | 0.0148 | 0.0039 |
| `column_id_dim` curve only (§104's five) | 5 | **0.0072** | 0.0077 | 0.0088 |

Read honestly. On all 12 rows the surrogates beat the mean, but that is the architecture
jump (+0.05 AP, §172) being learnt from a yes/no feature, not a sweep prediction. Within the
cell-attention rows the GBM edges the mean (0.0039 against 0.0069, from rows that differ in
context and volume, which it can split on). On the question 48.16 actually asks, the
`column_id_dim` curve, **neither surrogate beats the mean predictor** (§149 compared only the
two surrogates; the mean was not in its table). Worst row for the mean and the GBM is
`column_id_dim` 16, the peak.

## Predictions for untried `column_id_dim` (ESTIMATED, cell attention, ctx 1,000, 48k tasks)

| `column_id_dim` | ridge | GBM | GBM bootstrap SD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 0.1953 | 0.1938 | 0.0039 |
| 10 | 0.1954 | 0.1938 | 0.0028 |
| 14 | 0.1956 | 0.1938 | 0.0028 |
| 18 | 0.1958 | 0.2053 | 0.0031 |
| 28 | 0.1964 | 0.1926 | 0.0037 |
| 40 | 0.1971 | 0.1893 | 0.0043 |

The ridge is flat (no usable curve). The GBM is piecewise constant, so its values at 8, 10
and 14 are just the 12 arm's value, and 18 takes a value between 16 and 20 only because of
where a tree split falls. These are interpolation artefacts of the method, not findings about
the model.

## Which point a real run should check

**`column_id_dim` = 18**, the one untried value where the two surrogates disagree most
(0.2053 against 0.1958, difference 0.0095), and next to the peak, where a wrong surrogate would
change the choice of 16.

What the check can and cannot say. One run's seed SD of the five-fold mean is about 0.0039 to
0.0055 and two runs differ by up to 0.0117 at 95% (§173). The surrogates' disagreement (0.0095)
is below that bar, so a single run at 18 cannot decide between them; it can only show whether
the GBM's 0.2053 is plausible. §173's three fp32 re-trains at `column_id_dim` 16 scored 0.1947,
0.1858 and 0.1845 under default inference, below the published checkpoint's 0.2072 in the
§84 regime, so the peak itself may partly be a favourable seed. The result a run at 18 will
most likely return is "within noise of everything".

Comparison rules fixed in advance (not run): score the new checkpoint in the same regime as the
12 rows (full panel, five folds, `n_ensemble=8`, ctx 1,000, `configs/cellattn-v4-validation.yaml`).
Treat the surrogate as supported only if the measured mean AP lies within 0.0117 of 0.2053 AND
exceeds ridge's 0.1958 by more than 0.0039; anything else is "undecided", not a refutation.

## ESTIMATED cost (not launched)

Recipe: `scripts/kaggle/ts_e1_v48_s0` pattern (published recipe, fp16, split attention, DDP on a
Kaggle T4x2, 6,000 steps at batch 4 per rank = 48,000 tasks) with `--column-id-dim 18` appended
to `ARM_FLAGS`.

- MEASURED for the same recipe at `column_id_dim` 16: training 3,150 s (§171); whole kernel,
  training plus its in-kernel scoring, 5,402 s and 5,644 s (`runs/kaggle-ts-e1-v48-s{0,1}`),
  so about 1.5 to 1.6 T4x2 session-hours.
- ESTIMATED for 18: the same, since parameter count changes by about 0.01% (§104).
- Published fp32 single-GPU protocol instead: kernels took 13,030 to 13,523 s, about 3.6 to 3.8
  hours (`runs/kaggle-ts-pub-s{0,1,2}`).
- Not costed: re-scoring in the §84 regime (`n_ensemble=8`) on this Mac's CPU, because the
  in-kernel scoring uses packaged defaults and so is not comparable to the 12 rows. No timing
  of that scoring was found, so I give none.

A second seed at 16 in the same fp16 recipe would be needed to read the result against noise
(the §171 pair, 0.1975 and 0.1878, was scored under default inference, not the §84 regime).
That doubles the training cost to about 3 hours of T4x2 plus Mac scoring.
