## Reading

- The checkpoint picks out the true column(s) in 30 of 30 runs (exact top-k match), including
  the two-column task and including negated sign (`-x_3`), at every context size from 32 rows.
  True columns carry 0.84 to 1.00 of the positive ablation importance.
- Its AUC is at or just below logistic regression's in most cells (gap up to 0.075 AUC at
  32 rows, `x_2`; under 0.01 at 512 rows) and above it only for `-x_3` at 128 rows
  (0.992 against 0.985). Both are below the oracle's 1.000. So the shortfall is in the use of
  the column, not in finding it.
- The untrained control sits at 0.36 to 0.65 AUC, so the checkpoint's result is not the
  architecture alone. The control's 0.36 to 0.39 on `-x_3` is below 0.5: an untrained net can
  carry a fixed feature-to-score orientation.
- On `x_2 + x_5` at 128 and 512 rows the single arg-max is column 5 in both seeds; column 2 is
  still second, so top-2 is exact. Importance is split unevenly between the two columns.

## Limits

Two seeds per cell and one permutation per column: the AUC means have no reliable error bar
and the 2/2 counts are small. Row-permutation importance on a model that reads columns
jointly can understate or shift weight between correlated roles; the columns here are
independent. Attention weights were not extracted (the Verify clause allows either measure).
Thresholds at zero give about balanced classes; skewed base rates are not covered.
